# L002: finite local-prime correction (exploratory hypothesis)

No fitted parameters and no observed counts. The fixed truncation at 997 is a
computational convention, not a fitted optimum. This is a small extension of
the specified N0, with substantial prior overlap (Shanks--Kravitz 1967).

For a prime r>47, condition on p being uniformly distributed among nonzero
residues mod r. If r divides k, 2kp+1 never vanishes. Otherwise exactly one
of the r-1 possible p classes is excluded. Dividing these survival fractions
by the unconditional prime survival factor (r-1)/r gives r/(r-1) when r|k,
and r(r-2)/(r-1)^2 otherwise. Multiply N0 by these factors for 53<=r<=997.
This is the finite Bateman--Horn local correction beyond N0's explicit sieve.

N0's 1/k residue heuristic assumes uniformity inside the quadratic residues
allowed by L1. L3 proves the group-theoretic equivalence, not this uniformity
conditional on p prime. Kummer-field entanglement and all primes beyond 997
remain unresolved. The exceptional p=r cases also limit small-range accuracy.
There is no established global constant Lambda, asymptotic theorem, novelty
claim, or promised improvement over N0. Failure is retained, not edited away.

Poisson exponent multiplicities are an additional independence approximation.
Summary cells overlap; composite deviance is not an independent likelihood.
Maximal multiplicity is reported as an expected maximum, not scored by a
Poisson count likelihood. The census hash is a verification target, not a
predictable statistical cell. Vault-S scoring is reserved for the operator.
