# L001 — zero-fit Kummer/Bateman–Horn census predictor

Status: preregistration candidate; **overlap-found**, not a novel formula. Wagstaff (1983), equation (1), already gives the same factor weight. This campaign will test that established heuristic on explicitly committed finite cells. No observed counts, fitted constants, lookup tables of observations or tuning to vault data are used.

## Formula and local factors

For prime p and k>=1, define A(p,k)=1 when k=0 or -p mod 4, zero otherwise, and

    w(p,k) = A(p,k) * 2 C2/k * product_{odd prime r|k}(r-1)/(r-2) / log(2kp).

Here C2=product_{r>2 prime}(1-1/(r-1)^2)=0.6601618158468696... is the twin-prime constant. The model predicts a census count by summing these weights over its actual prime exponents and multipliers. Natural logarithms; inclusive endpoints. This is an averaged density, not a claim that a deterministic individual pair has that probability. The law does not sieve actual q for small primes; doing so while retaining the averaged BH factors would count the conditioning twice.

The two-polynomial BH system is f1(t)=t, f2(t)=2kt+1. For r=2 its product has one root, contributing 2. For odd r|k it has one root, contributing r/(r-1). For odd r not dividing k it has two distinct roots, contributing r(r-2)/(r-1)^2. Dividing the latter product into the former gives 2 C2 times the displayed divisor correction. Conditioning on the observed prime p replaces dt/log t by the actual prime sum; the remaining logarithm is log(2kp). This is an explicit modelling discretization, not a consequence of BH for each individual p.

## Kummer/Chebotarev step and quadratic entanglement

Put n=2k and E=Q(zeta_lcm(n,8)). Since sqrt(2) is in Q(zeta_8), adjoining 2^(1/n) to E has degree n/2. The rank-one radical/cyclotomic compatibility is the quadratic character of conductor 8 (Moree–Stevenhagen, Theorem 2.5). In the coset for a rational prime q=1 mod n, the action on sqrt(2) is forced by q mod 8. If q is 3 or 5 mod 8, the radical condition is impossible. If q is 1 or 7 mod 8, the compatible radical actions number n/2, and exactly one fixes 2^(1/n); their uniform fraction is 2/n=1/k. This is the origin of A(p,k)/k. The same reasoning can be phrased over Q(zeta_n,2^(1/n)): its degree is n phi(n), halved when 8 divides n, with the remaining conductor-8 compatibility imposed by L1.

For prime p and prime q=2kp+1, being an n-th power is equivalent to ord_q(2)=p by L3. Thus no unrestricted near-primitive-root inclusion–exclusion or Artin constant should be multiplied into this expression: the prime quotient p has already fixed the order alternatives. Chebotarev describes the Frobenius fraction for fixed fields among rational primes. **Combining that fraction with the extra prime-value condition on (q-1)/(2k) is a joint equidistribution heuristic. It is not proved by Chebotarev or GRH alone.** Uniformity as k grows also remains unproved.

## Explicit aggregate constant

For an odd r, the average multiplier correction is 1+(1/r)*((r-1)/(r-2)-1)=1+1/(r(r-2)), the reciprocal of its C2 Euler factor. Their product cancels C2. Odd k are allowed for half the prime exponent classes; k divisible by 4 are allowed for both, and k=2 mod 4 are forbidden. Their densities give 2*(1/2*1/2+1/4)=1. Thus the coarse logarithmic-average constant Lambda is **1.000000**. This is an algebraic cancellation in the heuristic, not a new theorem or a finite-range replacement for the exact S(k) correction. The unaveraged prefactor 2C2 is **1.320324** to six decimals.

## Numerical convention (frozen before computation)

The source uses log(2kp), as in Wagstaff's formula. Its difference from log(q) is at most a relative 1/(2*lo*log(2*lo)) in these cells; neither convention is fitted. Thirty-two fixed Chebyshev nodes in log p evaluate the finite prime sum. These are interpolation coefficients generated from the theoretical function, not parameters fitted to observations. The nearest pole of 1/(log(2k)+log p) is outside the interpolation interval. Precommit tests compare against direct summation on multiple observed domains and compare 16/32/48-node evaluation on the prediction domain (using exponents only, never factor tests). No tolerances are changed after observations.

Counts in all dyadic buckets and k mod 3,4,5,8,12 classes are predicted. Per-exponent Poisson means are the weight sums; ge1 and ge2 use 1-exp(-mu) and 1-(1+mu)exp(-mu). Maximum is the expected maximum of independent Poisson variables, from its CDF product, truncating at 32 with an explicit tail bound. **Poissonity and independence are further hypotheses**, especially with small k, not established by the mean law. The census hash is a verification identifier, not an inferable numeric count; no SHA-256 hash forecast is asserted.
